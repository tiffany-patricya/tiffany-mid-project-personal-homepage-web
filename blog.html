My Experience on Debating (NUDC)

I participated for the Faculty of Engineering NUDC's Internal Selection and got the 4th best speaker. That makes me continue on the University Internal Selection and being one of the Faculty of Engineering delegates. I partnered up with Samuel. And here's what can I share about debating, based on my experience.

Debate gets a bad reputation. People often associate it with arguing, being aggressive, or trying to "win" at all costs. But anyone who has actually practiced debate knows it's the complete opposite — it's one of the most disciplined, thoughtful, and empathetic skills we can develop.

I've been involved in debate for a while now, and the biggest thing it taught me is how to listen. Real debate isn't about waiting for your turn to talk — it's about genuinely understanding the other side so you can engage with it meaningfully. You can't refute an argument you haven't actually heard.

Here are a few tips I've picked up along the way:

Know your argument inside out. Confidence comes from preparation, not volume.

Anticipate the other side. The best debaters already know what the opposition will say — and have a response ready.

Stay calm. Emotions are the enemy of clarity. The moment you get flustered, you lose the room.

Use evidence, not just opinion. A well-placed statistic or example is worth ten passionate sentences.

Concede gracefully when you're wrong. Acknowledging a good point from the other side actually makes you look stronger, not weaker.

Whether you're presenting to a class, negotiating with a friend, or navigating a difficult conversation, debate skills translate everywhere. Honestly, I think it should be a required subject in every school.

My Experience in ONMIPA PT

Joining the university-level selection for ONMIPA Biology as a representative from the engineering faculty was a new and challenging experience for me. Even though I had participated in many competitions back in high school, this felt completely different—it was my first time competing at this level, and I couldn't help but feel nervous.

However, this experience taught me a lot. Instead of feeling discouraged by the results, I found myself becoming even more motivated. It made me realize how much more I can learn and improve. This journey has sparked a stronger determination in me to study harder, prepare better, and come back more ready for the next opportunity next year.

Is AI Making Us Smarter Or Just Lazier?

Let's be honest — AI is everywhere right now. From generating images and writing essays to helping doctors diagnose diseases and assisting engineers in writing code, artificial intelligence has quietly embedded itself into almost every corner of our daily lives. And as someone studying Informatics Engineering, I find myself thinking about this a lot... A LOT.

On one hand, AI tools are genuinely impressive. They save time, reduce repetitive tasks, and make information more accessible than ever. I've used AI to help me understand complex concepts, debug code, and even brainstorm ideas when I'm stuck. It feels like having a very patient, very knowledgeable study buddy available 24/7.

But here's my honest take — I think we need to be careful. There's a real difference between using AI as a tool and using it as a replacement for thinking. When we stop wrestling with problems ourselves and just outsource every challenge to an AI, we lose something important: the ability to think critically and independently.

AI is a powerful tool, not a crutch. The people who will thrive in this era aren't the ones who rely on AI the most — they're the ones who know how to use it wisely. And that, I think, is a skill worth developing intentionally.

Arduino-Based Automatic Saving System

In our Computer Architecture group project, we developed an Arduino-based automatic saving system. The idea was to create a smart piggy bank that can detect the color of banknotes and automatically update the total balance.

By integrating sensors with Arduino, the system is able to identify different denominations based on color detection, then process and display the updated amount in real time. This project not only strengthened our understanding of hardware-software integration but also showed how simple technologies can be applied to solve everyday problems in a creative way.
